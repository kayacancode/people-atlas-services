People Data Sync & the optional People service

Privacy policy

Last updated: November 20, 2026

This policy explains how People Data Sync, maintained by Kaya Jones, accesses, uses, stores, and shares data. It covers the Obsidian desktop plugin and the optional hosted People graph service linked from this site. Different features involve different data flows; opening these information pages does not connect your Google account.

Google information we access and why

  • Account identity: Google sign-in supplies your identity, including your email address, to establish your session and associate data with your account.
  • Gmail: normal desktop plugin sync reads message metadata such as senders, recipients, subject lines, dates, and thread structure to create contact records and estimate relationship activity. The gmail.readonly grant also permits reading message bodies; normal desktop plugin sync does not fetch them. The hosted app has additional optional body-processing features described below.
  • Calendar: read-only event access supports meeting history and relationship activity. Event details can include dates, titles, and attendees. Optional booking connects separately and requests event-management and free/busy access to find available times and create authorized calendar events.
  • Contacts: read-only Google Contacts and Other Contacts access supplies contact names, photos, and profile information.

The desktop plugin currently requests Gmail, Calendar read access, and Contacts read access together, including when you do not use every feature. Optional hosted booking and agent integrations have separate authorization steps. Review the permissions displayed by Google and the in-product request before enabling a feature.

Optional hosted email context

When you enable ongoing email context for a connected account, the hosted service can retrieve eligible incoming message bodies during subsequent syncs and use Workers AI to extract relationship facts and limited quotations. This processing continues while the feature is enabled; it does not ask for confirmation for each message. Separately enabled owner-lens learning can read eligible sent-message bodies to infer your current focus, needs, and offers. Extracted facts, source references, and excerpts can be stored as relationship or owner context and used by the configured context services.

The hosted app also offers explicitly confirmed, bounded deeper retrieval for a selected person and time window. Raw bodies from that retrieval are processed transiently rather than retained as complete messages in the graph database. Derived facts or excerpts may remain as context.

Use the email-context and owner-lens controls to stop ongoing collection. Disabling an account’s email context clears its stored email-context extractions. For owner-lens history, use the available deletion/reset controls or contact support; a remote processor’s cleanup can be pending or retried. Disconnecting or revoking Google access stops future access but is not, by itself, a guarantee that all previously derived context has been erased.

Other information you choose to provide

You may add people notes, company notes, relationship feedback, goals, meeting context, graph snapshots, shared network contributions, and optional connected-service information such as Granola meeting notes. Features use this context to organize relationships, display evidence, surface possible reasons to reconnect, and support the actions you request. Scores and inferred connections are estimates, not proof of closeness or willingness to make an introduction.

Local storage and optional cloud storage

The Obsidian plugin stores contact indexes, people notes, settings, OAuth tokens, and API keys in your vault. These local credentials are not encrypted by the plugin. Your vault sync or backup provider may copy those files to other devices or services.

Using the hosted People app stores the connected account’s authorized data in Cloudflare-hosted storage, including Workers Durable Objects and D1. Imported Gmail metadata, calendar information, contacts, and relationship context are cloud data when you connect them to the hosted app. Cloud refresh grants are encrypted before storage. Session cookies support sign-in; browser-to-Obsidian handoffs use short-lived connection requests, and embedded sessions can last up to 24 hours.

Opening the hosted graph from Obsidian does not itself upload your vault. An explicit graph push sends the fields included in that graph snapshot. A separately configured score endpoint receives the contact scores and account identity you choose to send.

Sharing, agents, and optional AI

Contributing data to a shared network or sharing a graph makes the selected information available to the authorized viewers of that network or share. Joining, importing, pushing, and sharing are distinct choices. An integration or personal agent can access only the features and context authorized for that integration; review its access before connecting it.

Optional desktop AI enrichment sends the selected existing person note—including meeting history or other text in that note—mapped relationships, connected-person names, and Gmail statistics or recent subjects to Anthropic using your API key. Hosted context and suggestion features can process relevant authorized context using Cloudflare Workers AI, Jev/Typesafe, or a configured context service such as Honcho. Connected services apply their own data policies. Enable these features only for data you intend to process with those services.

Introduction, booking, and scheduling features act on the requests or permissions you provide. A suggestion is not consent to contact someone. Scheduling may create calendar events or send service emails when you authorize the corresponding workflow.

Limits on use of Google data

Google user data is used to provide the user-facing relationship, sync, and connected features you authorize. We do not sell Google user data, use it for advertising, or use it to train generalized AI models. Transfers to processors support those features, security, or applicable legal requirements. People Data Sync’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs is subject to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Retention, disconnecting, and deletion

Local notes and indexes remain in your vault until you remove them. Disabling or uninstalling the plugin does not automatically erase the notes it created. To remove local plugin data, disable the plugin, remove its .obsidian/plugins/gmail-crm folder, and delete the generated people or company notes you no longer want. Consider copies held by your own sync and backup providers.

Disconnecting an account in the hosted app stops its sync and removes that account’s imported records from the active account store. User-authored notes, shared contributions, or separate connected-service records may require separate removal. Revoking Google access stops future API access but is not a request to erase all previously imported data. For deletion of hosted data or help identifying retained records, contact kaya@betaworks.com. We do not promise immediate removal from provider backups or records we must retain for legal or security purposes.

Your controls

You can disconnect integrations, stop sharing, disable optional enrichment, and remove local data. You can also revoke Google access from your Google Account connections. Look for the application name displayed on the consent screen; it may still use an earlier People name while branding is updated.

Infrastructure and contact

Cloudflare provides hosting, storage, and network infrastructure and may process operational request and security logs. Google provides the APIs and sign-in services you authorize. The app does not include a product analytics or advertising tracker. For questions, private data requests, or concerns about this policy, contact Kaya Jones at kaya@betaworks.com. Please do not post tokens, private messages, or account exports in public issues.

When data practices change, this page will be updated. A new use of Google data requires the appropriate disclosure and authorization before it is enabled.
